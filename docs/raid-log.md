## Prospective risk assessment framework

| Rating | Likelihood | Impact if the risk occurs |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Evidence supports dependencies being met and controls working; occurrence remains possible but appears unlikely. | Localized disruption manageable within existing capacity and timing, without materially affecting required deliverables or execution. |
| Medium | Evidence is mixed or identifies a credible vulnerability; occurrence is plausible, but not clearly expected. | Material rework or cross-functional recovery is needed to protect deliverables, data quality, site/participant execution, or milestone forecasts. |
| High | Evidence shows deteriorating conditions or ineffective controls, making occurrence likely unless further action succeeds. | A required readiness gate, milestone forecast, critical deliverable, data integrity, or participant/site execution would be materially compromised. |

Insufficient evidence means **Not yet rated**, with missing evidence identified; it does not default to Medium. No numerical probabilities or quantitative thresholds are assumed. An event that has occurred is managed as an issue, not a prospective risk.

Priority is derived using the approved Option A matrix, for both inherent and current residual assessments:

| Likelihood / Impact | Low | Medium | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | Low | Low | Medium |
| Medium | Low | Medium | High |
| High | Medium | High | High |

If either input is Not yet rated, priority is Not yet rated. Inherent exposure is assessed before controls. Current residual exposure may credit only controls evidenced as implemented and operating. Planned or proposed mitigation is tracked separately and is not treated as effective. Reassess residual exposure only when evidence establishes implementation and operation of the relevant control/mitigation.

Risk ratings are separate from issue status, milestone delivery health, and escalation triggers. A milestone may remain On Track while a prospective risk needs monitoring. This log makes no change to Step 3 health classifications.

- Step 4 attendance remains Lead + CRO Lead as the weekly core; DM, Sites and other functions join when relevant. Required written input/approval or authorized delegation may substitute for attendance; absence is never consent. If sufficient input/authority is unavailable, defer only the affected decision.
- Functional owners attempt resolution within authority; the Lead coordinates unresolved cross-functional matters; escalate to Sponsor if resolution fails or Sponsor-level authority is needed. Risk identification alone does not require Sponsor involvement.

- Close a prospective risk only when exposure ends, or residual risk is formally accepted by the appropriate existing authority. Record the closure basis and evidence; for acceptance, document authority, acceptance and rationale. Low residual priority alone is insufficient. Neither current risk has acceptance or closure evidence.
- Continuing controls retain their established owners after risk closure and do not stop unless established study requirements separately support that change. No new approval authority is created.
